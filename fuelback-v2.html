Why Recovery Matters

The best recovery plan depends on the workout, not just one fixed formula. Here's what's actually happening when you refuel.

Carbs = Energy Refill

Carbohydrates refill glycogen, your muscles' stored energy used during hard practices and games.

Protein = Repair Support

Protein helps repair and rebuild muscle tissue that gets stressed during training.

Hydration = Performance

Hydration affects performance, fatigue, and how ready you feel the next day.

Timing = Next-Day Readiness

When you eat after practice matters most when your next session is close.

Total daily food intake matters — one good recovery snack can't fully fix a whole day of under-eating. The ratio is a tool, not a magic number. Use it when conditions actually require it.

Step 02 · Match the Day

Pick Your Training Day

Not every practice deserves the same plan. Here's how to scale your recovery to match the workload.

— Level 01

Light Day

Short skill practice, low-intensity workout, light lift.

Main advice: Normal meals, fluids, and consistency. Don't overthink it.

Ratio needed? Usually no
— Level 02

Moderate Day

Normal practice, moderate lift, regular team practice.

Main advice: Balanced meal or snack with carbs and protein within an hour or two.

Ratio needed? Helpful but not always
— Level 03

Hard Day / Short Recovery

Long practice, tournament, two-a-day, game tomorrow, intense conditioning.

Main advice: Use a 3:1 or 4:1 recovery snack if a full meal is delayed.

Ratio needed? Most useful here

The Evidence

The Research Behind FuelBack

Here's the literature in plain English. Sources are listed at the bottom of the page.

01

Carbs Are the Foundation

Carbohydrate availability is the foundation of repeated performance. If carbs are low, the rest of the plan struggles.

02

3:1 and 4:1 Ratios Help

3:1 and 4:1 carb-to-protein ratios can help when carbs are low or recovery time is short between sessions.

03

Protein Has Limits

Adding protein is less clearly beneficial when carbohydrate and calorie intake are already adequate for the day.

04

Hydration Stands Alone

Hydration and electrolytes affect performance and recovery separately from food intake — they aren't interchangeable.

05

Realistic Routines Win

Student athletes need realistic routines because school schedules, transportation, and homework make ideal recovery harder.

06

Individualized > Universal

The best recommendation is individualized recovery planning — not one universal formula applied to everyone.

"The ratio is a tool, not a magic number."

Use it when it fits. Build consistent daily habits everywhere else.

For Programs

Coach & Team Use

For coaches, athletic trainers, and school programs. Use this to normalize recovery as part of team culture.

Team Recovery Checklist

  • Did athletes eat before practice today?
  • Is there a recovery snack available after practice?
  • Is water available during and after practice?
  • Are athletes fueling differently on hard days versus light days?
  • Are any athletes showing signs of fatigue, soreness, or low energy?
  • Do athletes know what to eat when a full meal is delayed?
  • Is the team hydrating before and during practice, not just after?

How to Build the Habit

  • Normalize post-practice fueling as part of the cool-down routine.
  • Suggest easy team snacks: chocolate milk, bananas, granola bars, peanut butter crackers.
  • Remind athletes the day before to bring a recovery snack.
  • Track next-day readiness with a quick energy/soreness check-in.
  • Differentiate plans for hard practice days vs. light/skill days.
  • Frame recovery nutrition as performance strategy, not extra effort.

Easy Team Snack Ideas

  • Individual chocolate milk cartons — pre-portioned, easy to store
  • Peanut butter cracker packs and fruit cups
  • Banana and string cheese combinations
  • Granola bars paired with a water bottle
  • Bagels with individual peanut butter packets
  • Cereal cups with shelf-stable milk

Signs of Under-Fueling

  • Consistent fatigue that doesn't improve with sleep
  • Unusual soreness that lasts multiple days
  • Declining performance across a training week
  • Athletes skipping meals during the school day
  • Difficulty concentrating during practice
  • Frequent illness or longer recovery from minor injuries
